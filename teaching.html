Teaching

My teaching introduces students to anthropology as a way of questioning familiar assumptions and connecting everyday experience to broader structures of power, history, and inequality. I encourage students to approach social worlds with curiosity, analytical rigor, and attention to the ethical demands of understanding lives different from their own.

Courses Taught

Introduction to Cultural Anthropology

Bucknell University

An introductory course examining culture, social difference, kinship, religion, political economy, globalization, and inequality through ethnographic scholarship.

Anthropology and Global Encounters

Syracuse University

A course exploring colonialism, globalization, migration, exchange, and cultural transformation through anthropological approaches to cross-cultural encounters.

Critical Issues for the United States

Syracuse University

A course examining contemporary public issues and the responsibilities of democratic citizenship. Through topics including public education, economic inequality, and immigration, students consider tensions among liberty, equality, and inclusion while strengthening their critical reading, reasoning, writing, speaking, and deliberative skills.

Pedagogical Approach

I use ethnographic exercises, collaborative discussion, and scaffolded writing assignments to help students develop analytical confidence. My courses emphasize careful observation, positionality, ethical research, and the relationship between local worlds and global processes.

I also encourage students to treat writing as a process of inquiry. Short reflective exercises, close engagement with ethnographic texts, and structured revisions help students move from description to interpretation and from opinion to evidence-based argument.
